ChromiumOS, pried open.

NickelOS is a fork of ChromiumOS, built on the ChromeOS you know from Chromebooks, that pries open the Linux underneath it, revealing Gentoo. Every Linux app just works, because it's just Linux! No need for Crostini or any sort of virtual machine.

What changes

NickelOS is based on ChromeOS, with added fixes, features and enhancements that give you the same tools and features as any other Linux distro!

Gentoo underneath

The whole system is a current Gentoo, not a container or a VM: one glibc, Gentoo's Python, Gentoo's Mesa. sudo emerge installs anything in Gentoo's tree or GURU, with your own USE flags, from a binary package when there's one and from source when there isn't. Your services run under OpenRC. An OS update replaces the system and leaves what you installed alone.

Native Linux apps

Linux apps draw straight on Exo, the Wayland server built into ChromeOS's desktop, and X11 apps go through Xwayland with the GPU. They're in the launcher and on the shelf, and their windows snap, go into overview and move between desks like the browser's.

Games and GPUs

NVIDIA cards run on NVIDIA's own driver, AMD and Intel on Mesa. Steam, Proton and Wine run as they do on other distros, and controllers work. ChromeOS normally blocks pointer lock for Linux apps; NickelOS allows it, so mouse look works in games, and Discord's push-to-talk works while you play.

New on the desktop

Things ChromeOS never had. Icons on the desktop, with previews of your photos and videos. A screen saver of your own pictures, with the weather if you want it. OneDrive, Dropbox, Proton Drive or Nextcloud in Files, and the computer's other drives too, Windows included. Print Screen opens whichever screenshot app you pick.

Phones and Android apps

Android apps run through Waydroid. Install them from an APK, with no Google Play and no Google account behind them, and they show up in the launcher like any other app. Your phone pairs through KDE Connect instead of Google's Phone Hub, to ring it, send it files and see its battery. That part is still being finished.

Still ChromeOS

Verified boot, a read-only system on A/B partitions, and encrypted homes, all straight from ChromiumOS. If an update won't boot, the computer goes back to the version before by itself. The shelf, launcher, Settings and Files work the way they do on a Chromebook.

Apps

Linux apps get ChromeOS's dialogs

When a Linux app asks the desktop for a file, the camera or the screen, ChromeOS answers. NickelOS connects the two with its own portal backend, xdg-desktop-portal-nickel, as GNOME and KDE each have theirs.

Files
ChromeOS's own picker, to open and to save.
Camera and location
Flatpak apps ask through ChromeOS's dialogs before they get either. ChromeOS's location switch covers every Linux app.
Screen sharing
ChromeOS's picker, with its sharing your screen notice and a Stop button.
Notifications
ChromeOS notifications, with the app's name, icon and buttons.
Shortcuts and tray icons
Push-to-talk and other global shortcuts, after asking. Tray icons sit on the shelf.
Printing
Your printers and Save as PDF.
Passwords
Saved passwords go in a keyring that unlocks when you sign in. pkexec and other admin prompts use ChromeOS's own password dialog.
Typing
Chinese, Korean and Japanese input in Linux apps, old X11 ones included.
Staying awake
Videos, games and downloads keep the screen on or the machine awake while they need it.

De-Googled by default

Local accounts, no Google services

Your account
Setup works offline and makes a local account. It's a real Linux user, and the first one on the device is an administrator, with sudo.
Setup
Pick Full experience for DuckDuckGo, Proton's apps, uBlock Origin and a set of free apps, or Minimal for none of it.
Google services
None built in: no Google AI features, no Privacy Sandbox, no promotions, no content recommendations. Search suggestions stay off unless you turn them on.
Cloud storage
Add OneDrive, Dropbox, Proton Drive, Nextcloud or Google Drive in Settings, and they show up in Files.

Installing apps

Where apps come from

Bazaar, a store for Flathub, is pinned to the shelf. AppImages run from your home folder. For everything else there's Gentoo's package tree.

you@nickelos ~ $ eix gnome-text-editor
you@nickelos ~ $ sudo emerge --ask app-editors/gnome-text-editor
you@nickelos ~ $ flatpak install flathub com.valvesoftware.Steam
you@nickelos ~ $

Why NickelOS

We like ChromeOS's desktop. It's quick and stays out of the way. What we didn't like was that on a Chromebook your Linux apps run in a Debian VM on a computer that's already running Linux, and that the machine wants a Google account before it's yours. NickelOS keeps the desktop and drops both.

  • Non-commercial
  • Open source from the first release
  • Community project

The long run

Ready for 2034

Google's priority is now Googlebook OS, its new laptop system built on Android, and ChromeOS updates end in mid-2034. Until then Google is expected to keep shipping security fixes and new features for ChromeOS, and NickelOS plans to follow each ChromiumOS release, kernel included.

After 2034, NickelOS plans to keep the ChromiumOS desktop going on the Gentoo it's already built on, with Gentoo's kernels and security fixes and our own feature updates.
